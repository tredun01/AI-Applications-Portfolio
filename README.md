Artificial Intelligence Portfolio

Trevion Duncan

Welcome to my ITAI 2372 Artificial Intelligence Portfolio. This repository contains coursework, assignments, reflections, and research completed throughout the course.

The portfolio explores how Artificial Intelligence is being developed and applied across multiple industries, including **financial services, agriculture, entertainment and media, retail, and supply chain management**. It also examines emerging AI technologies, future trends, ethical considerations, and the challenges organizations may face as AI becomes more widely adopted.


About This Portfolio

The purpose of this portfolio is to showcase what I learned throughout ITAI 2372 and demonstrate my ability to analyze how Artificial Intelligence can be applied to real-world problems.

Throughout the course, I explored both the **benefits and challenges of AI**, including automation, efficiency, data analysis, prediction, privacy, fairness, cybersecurity, job displacement, and human oversight.


Key Areas of Study

Artificial Intelligence & Future Trends

I examined recent advancements in AI and how the technology could develop in the future. Topics included:

* Generative AI
* Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)
* AI in healthcare
* AI in business
* AI in space exploration
* AI regulation
* Privacy
* Misinformation
* Fairness and responsible AI

AI is becoming increasingly integrated into everyday life and professional environments, while future developments could expand the capabilities and applications of AI even further.


AI in Financial Services

One of my assignments focused on **AI in fraud detection**.

I explored how financial institutions can use AI and machine learning to analyze large numbers of transactions, identify unusual activity, and assign risk scores to potentially fraudulent transactions.

Key concepts:

* Fraud detection
* Machine learning
* Transaction monitoring
* Pattern recognition
* Risk scoring
* Data analysis

 I also examined challenges including false positives, data quality, privacy concerns, bias, and implementation costs.

 AI in Agriculture

I analyzed how AI is being used to improve modern agriculture through technologies such as:

* Precision farming
* Drones
* Sensors
* Satellite imagery
* Crop monitoring
* Automated irrigation
* Autonomous farming

These technologies can help farmers make data-driven decisions, reduce waste, monitor crops, and improve efficiency.

I also explored ethical concerns such as job displacement, farm-data privacy, unequal access to technology, and transparency in AI decision-making.



AI in Entertainment & Media

Another area I explored was the impact of AI on the **music and entertainment industry**.

Topics included:

* AI-generated music
* AI-assisted music production
* Stem separation
* Automated mixing and mastering
* Music recommendations
* Personalized playlists
* Copyright
* Artist rights
* Job displacement

AI is creating new opportunities for musicians and creators while also raising important questions about copyright, compensation, creativity, and the use of artists' work as training data.


AI in Retail & Supply Chain

I also explored how AI is being used in retail and supply chain operations.

Applications include:

* Demand forecasting
* Inventory optimization
* Delivery route optimization
* Supplier monitoring
* Supply chain risk prediction
* Warehouse automation

AI can help businesses predict customer demand, reduce waste, improve delivery efficiency, and identify potential disruptions before they occur.

This assignment also examined privacy, cybersecurity, job displacement, transparency, and the importance of keeping humans involved in important decisions.

Key Takeaways

One of my biggest takeaways from this course is that **AI is much broader than chatbots and online assistants**.

AI can be used to analyze financial transactions, monitor crops, improve music production, predict customer demand, optimize inventory, and identify potential supply chain problems.

The course also showed me that successful AI adoption requires more than just technology. Organizations must consider **privacy, cybersecurity, fairness, transparency, human oversight, and the impact of automation on workers and communities**.
